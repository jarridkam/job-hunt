# Rivals Automate Blindly; Jobhunt Must Review Loudly

Across 2023-2026 reviews, complaint boards and comparison articles, the job-search tool market fails on **trust and upkeep, not on feature count**. Trackers (Teal, Huntr, Simplify, Careerflow) score 4.2-4.8 stars yet draw one dominant complaint: statuses, notes and follow-ups must be typed in by hand, so the tracker rots unless used daily ([wobo.ai](https://www.wobo.ai/blog/teal-review/); [PitchHired](https://pitchhired.com/blog/huntr-vs-teal)). Auto-apply bots (LazyApply 2.1/5, JobCopilot 1.8/5, Massive 1.7/5 on Trustpilot) fail on five structural axes: hallucinated form answers, applications to jobs the user excluded, duplicates, silent stalls, and submissions fired before the user could look ([Trustpilot LazyApply](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lazyapply.com); [Trustpilot JobCopilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/jobcopilot.com); [Trustpilot Massive](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/usemassive.com)). Billing dark patterns are the second-largest complaint category everywhere. Meanwhile **18-22% of postings are ghost jobs** (Greenhouse platform data) and US job-scam losses hit **$501M in 2024**, and no mainstream tool flags either ([Quartz](https://qz.com/one-in-five-job-postings-is-fake-ghost-jobs-1851738201); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/new-ftc-data-show-big-jump-reported-losses-fraud-125-billion-2024)). The whitespace is precise: inbox-driven status updates, assessment-invite detection (including spam-folder sweeps), calendar-synced deadlines with escalating alerts, follow-up nudges with drafted notes, honest fit explanations, review-by-default applying with receipts, and phone-first capture. Jobhunt already covers most of that whitespace; the plan below is therefore a delta: first make the front end presentable and loud about the right things (Phase 1), then harden encryption, auth, backups, export/delete, VPS, Playwright and apply pacing (Phase 2), while deliberately not building the autopilot, volume and metering features that users demonstrably resent.

## Users resent upkeep and silent wrong actions, not missing features

The tracker complaints are strikingly uniform. Teal's tracker "takes ongoing manual upkeep" and "can feel more like a spreadsheet than a workspace" ([wobo.ai](https://www.wobo.ai/blog/teal-review/); [Scoutify](https://scoutify.com/blog/teal-review)); Huntr's board "can become another admin system if you do not use the board daily" ([PitchHired](https://pitchhired.com/blog/huntr-vs-teal)); Simplify's own roundup concedes "application status updates are manual" ([Resumly](https://www.resumly.ai/best/best-job-application-trackers)). The only commercial inbox integration, Simplify's beta Gmail sync for Simplify+ users, is explicitly suggestion-only (Approve / Decline / Approve All), warns that initial sync "can take a long time," and never mentions assessments or coding tests ([Simplify help](https://help.simplify.jobs/articles/0236686-email-integration)). The feature request behind it has 21 votes and a user asking for a forward-to-alias design instead of full mailbox access on privacy grounds ([Simplify Featurebase](https://simplifyjobs.featurebase.app/p/email-integration-for-application-tracking)). The second tier of tracker complaints is small UX debt: no sort or filter by date posted or applied (Huntr, Oct 2026), notes hidden from the table view (Teal, Jul 2026), un-renamable statuses where "Rejected" is called demoralising (Simplify, 11 votes), and AI side panels that "jump in my face all the time" or cannot be dismissed (Huntr, Careerflow) ([Chrome Web Store Huntr](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/huntr-job-search-tracker/mihdfbecejheednfigjpdacgeilhlmnf/reviews); [Trustpilot Teal](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/tealhq.com); [Simplify Featurebase](https://simplifyjobs.featurebase.app/); [Chrome Web Store Careerflow](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/careerflow-ai-job-tracker/iadokddofjgcgjpjlfhngclhpmaelnli/reviews)). The third tier is trust: Teal lost a user's account and has a broken unsubscribe; Careerflow has "no way to delete your account" in two separate 1-star reviews; stability issues are extension-side (permissions lost after Chrome updates, duplicates from sync, onboarding "stuck 84%") ([wobo.ai](https://www.wobo.ai/blog/teal-review/); [Trustpilot Careerflow](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/careerflow.ai); [LoopCV guide](https://www.loopcv.pro/guides/simplify-jobs-not-working/)).

Auto-apply is a different category of failure. JobCopilot "said my reference was Jane Doe" and "generated false information on my résumé, including job titles and experience that I never had"; LazyApply set "yes" as the default on questions where it was wrong and could not enter a first name; AIHawk "sometimes added false information to résumés" and an earlier version hid the submitted résumé from the user ([Trustpilot JobCopilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/jobcopilot.com); [Trustpilot LazyApply](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lazyapply.com); [Business Insider via AOL](https://www.aol.com/free-ai-tool-help-apply-110301118.html)). Wrong-target applications are equally common: "continents and time zones that I did not select," "sales positions," on-site roles "NOWHERE near where I lived," emails "direct to CEOs and half weren't even open job reqs" ([Trustpilot JobCopilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/jobcopilot.com); [Trustpilot Massive](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/usemassive.com); [Trustpilot LoopCV](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/loopcv.pro)). Massive "automatically submitted 164 applications before I had a reasonable chance" to review; JobCopilot fired 20 in 15 minutes ([Trustpilot Massive](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/usemassive.com); [Trustpilot JobCopilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/jobcopilot.com)). Silence is its own failure: "It stopped applying altogether," "Always zero jobs," 12,000 matches but 14 applications ([Trustpilot JobCopilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/jobcopilot.com); [Trustpilot LoopCV](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/loopcv.pro)). The one first-person experiment that let three agents send **819 applications in a month got 5 interviews (0.6%)**, two with companies the author did not want, and he judged a non-AI approach would have done better ([Job Search with AI](https://jobsearchwithai.substack.com/p/i-let-ai-apply-to-819-jobs-for-me)). The revealing detail is that vendors who reply to reviews (Sonara, LoopCV) score better than those who do not (LazyApply, Massive) despite similar defect reports; responsiveness, not accuracy, drives the rating spread.

What users say they need to trust an agent maps one-to-one onto those failure classes. The most articulate statement is an unanswered AIHawk governance issue asking for an application limit, scope constraints on what documents the agent can reach, and a tamper-evident signed receipt per submission because otherwise "the only account of what was submitted, and where, is the agent's self-report" ([AIHawk #1158](https://github.com/feder-cr/Jobs_Applier_AI_Agent_AIHawk/issues/1158)). The observed workflow is "train then trust": full review for the first batch, then spot checks ([Job Search with AI](https://jobsearchwithai.substack.com/p/i-let-ai-apply-to-819-jobs-for-me)). A user who cancelled Massive "lost complete access to the account" and thus the record of what was sent in their name ([Trustpilot Massive](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/usemassive.com)). Billing complaints compound this: trial-to-paid conversion without a reminder is the single most repeated grievance across four vendors, JobCopilot's cancel button "only shows a loading animation," Jobscan has "no way for you to cancel directly from your account page," and refunds are conditioned on undisclosed application-count thresholds ([Trustpilot JobCopilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/jobcopilot.com); [Trustpilot Jobscan](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/jobscan.co); [Trustpilot Massive](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/usemassive.com)). Jobhunt has no billing, which removes an entire complaint class, but the underlying lesson transfers: never meter value by applications sent, never hide a cap, never act before the user has looked.

## Ghost jobs sit at one in five postings and scams at half a billion dollars, unflagged

The ghost-job numbers justify a legitimacy score as a first-class feature rather than a nicety. Greenhouse's own platform data puts **18-22% of postings per quarter as ghost jobs**, published with no hiring activity ([Quartz](https://qz.com/one-in-five-job-postings-is-fake-ghost-jobs-1851738201); [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/ghost-job-ads-are-one-reason-finding-a-new-role-can-be-soul-crushing-says-greenhouse-exec/)). Employer self-reports are worse: nearly one in three employers posted jobs with no current hiring intent (Clarify Capital, 1,000+ employers), 40% of 1,600 hiring managers posted a fake job in the past year (Resume Builder), and **81% of recruiters admit their companies post ghost jobs** (MyPerfectResume, fielded 2024) ([Jobstrack](https://jobstrack.io/blog/ghost-jobs-2026); [BenefitsPro](https://benefitspro.com/2024/11/27/the-2025-workforce-ghost-jobs-hr-anxiety--rto-tension/?amp=1)). A Columbia Law Review analysis of BLS JOLTS data found hires per ten postings fell from 8 in 2019 to 4 in 2024, with about 30% of openings never producing a hire, rising to ~48% in information/tech and ~60% in government ([Jobstrack](https://jobstrack.io/blog/ghost-jobs-2026)). On the seeker side, 67% have suspected a listing was fake, and the red flags they cite (typos 52%, vague company info 52%, too-good salary 45%, no salary 41%, requests for personal or financial details 39%, move off-platform 21%) read like a rules table ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/04/30/ghost-jobs-still-haunting-67-of-job-seekers-report-finds/)). Jobright's analysis of 4.4M applications estimates each ghost-job cycle costs a seeker about 9 hours ([Jobstrack](https://jobstrack.io/blog/ghost-jobs-2026)). The Congressional Research Service notes no official statistics exist, so all figures above are survey or platform-specific ([Jobstrack](https://jobstrack.io/blog/ghost-jobs-2026)).

Scams are growing faster than ghost jobs. FTC job and employment-agency scam losses rose from **$90M in 2020 to $501M in 2024**; Q4 2025 alone logged 25,002 reports and $150.4M lost with a $2,000 median ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/new-ftc-data-show-big-jump-reported-losses-fraud-125-billion-2024); [CW33](https://cw33.com/news/local/job-scams-result-in-150-4-million-losses-ftc-reports/)). BBB employment-scam reports doubled from 11,748 in 2024 to **23,234 in 2025**, with text-message recruitment about half of reports and task-based schemes surging; employment scams are the riskiest category for ages 18-34 ([WIBW](https://www.wibw.com/2026/05/31/bbb-reports-employment-scams-doubled-2025-task-based-schemes-surge/)). The failure case that matters most for an auto-applier is JobCopilot reportedly applying users to fraudulent listings, including one reviewer who nearly submitted "a W-4 and government ID to a fake company" ([Resumly](https://www.resumly.ai/best/best-job-application-bots)). No mainstream tracker or agent flags ghost or scam postings; the only detectors are indie heuristic tools (Ghost-Job Detector, jobghost.io, GhostCheck, an Apify URL monitor) and LinkedIn/Greenhouse "verified" badges whose mechanics are undocumented ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/ghost-job-detector/gpalkeokdmojnnlhgpelfnfmnffnkmhi); [jobghost.io](https://www.jobghost.io/); [AOL](https://www.aol.com/rise-ghost-jobs-one-five-180917662.html)). Jobhunt's points-based legitimacy score with reasons is therefore already a differentiator; the delta is to weight contact channel (text/WhatsApp/Telegram), payment or PII requests and off-platform redirects alongside posting age and reposts, because the losses skew toward those scam patterns rather than stale listings.

## Privacy risk is vendor buckets, shutdowns and LinkedIn bans, not hackers

No confirmed breach was found for any named tool in 2023-2026, but the adjacent space shows the shape of the risk: LiveCareer exposed **over 5.1 million resume files** from a misconfigured Azure container and TalentHook **~26 million resumes** from an open instance, both in 2025 ([Cybernews LiveCareer](https://cybernews.com/security/livecareer-resume-data-leak/); [Cybernews TalentHook](https://cybernews.com/security/talenthook-data-leak-exposes-millions/)). The structural concerns are in the policies: Simplify may present a user to recruiters "even if you haven't applied" and "shared" identifiers with Google and Meta for cross-context advertising; Teal's career content "may be used to improve AI models in anonymized, aggregated form" and disconnecting Google does not delete imported data; Sonara may share with "potential employers and recruiters, and others" and the original product shut down in February 2024, locking users out of their application history ([Simplify privacy](https://simplify.jobs/privacy); [Teal privacy](https://www.tealhq.com/privacy-policy); [Sonara privacy](https://www.sonara.ai/privacy-policy); [LoopCV blog](https://blog.loopcv.pro/what-happened-to-sonara/)). Simplify's extension is rated Medium risk (73/100) for cookies, webRequest and webNavigation permissions on `<all_urls>` ([CRXPlorer](https://crxplorer.com/extension/pbanhockgagggenencehbnadejlgchfc/simplify-copilot-autofill-job-applications-job-tracker-ai-resumes)), and a popular unrelated extension was sold and turned into spyware, the pattern that applies to any extension a job seeker installs ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3092299/my-most-useful-chrome-extension-was-stealing-my-data-for-years.html)).

Self-hosting removes the vendor-shutdown and recruiter-sharing risks but inherits the platform-ban risk. LinkedIn prohibits "any third party software" that automates activity and restricts accounts, re-enabling them "at the time specified on the suspension notification"; AIHawk's creator was restricted in September 2024, while a developer who paced his bot at one application every 5-10 minutes for four months was never contacted ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/90586); [Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/09/12/2024/linkedins-have-nots-and-have-bots)). Detection is moving to behavioural fingerprinting: screening questions completed faster than a human could, overnight volume spikes, repeated submissions from one device, identical navigation paths and resumes sharing phrasing ([Fingerprint](https://fingerprint.com/blog/job-application-fraud-detection/)). Greenhouse's Real Talent (June 2025) flags "spam, bot submissions, and applications with patterns that suggest fraud," and its 2025 survey of 4,136 respondents found 91% of US recruiters have spotted candidate deception and 34% spend up to half their week filtering spam ([Greenhouse Real Talent](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/greenhouse-real-talent-tm-launches-to-fix-overwhelming-candidate-pipelines-while-combatting-fraud-and-spam-in-hiring); [Greenhouse AI in Hiring](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair)). Recruiter backlash targets volume and sameness, not AI assistance: 39% of seekers now rewrite applications so they will not read as AI-generated ([Greenhouse candidate report](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/6-in-10-job-seekers-are-rethinking-how-they-write-to-avoid-sounding-like-ai-even-as-most-use-it-in-their-search)). For AI transparency, no retrieved tool labels which answers were AI-generated or shows confidence, and the highest-harm errors are in categorical compliance fields (visa, work authorization, demographics, salary) where LazyApply populated H-1B status wrong across a whole batch ([scale.jobs](https://scale.jobs/blog/lazyapply-risk-profile-banned-linkedin); [HireCarta](https://hirecarta.com/blog/jobright-review)).

For the backend itself, the sourced consensus for a single-user VPS is key-only SSH with no root login, default-deny firewall on 22/80/443, fail2ban, unattended upgrades, Caddy for automatic TLS and redirects, whole-file SQLCipher (~5-15% overhead) or per-field Fernet with MultiFernet rotation, keys kept out of the database, repo and environment variables, encrypted backups stored apart from their key, append-only audit logging, and Playwright run as non-root with the sandbox decision made explicitly because `chromiumSandbox` defaults to false and removing `--no-sandbox` does not enable it ([USAVPS](https://usavps.com/blog/post/vps-security-hardening-checklist-linux/); [Caddy docs](https://caddyserver.com/docs/automatic-https); [SQLCipher](https://www.zetetic.net/sqlcipher/about/); [cryptography.io Fernet](https://cryptography.io/en/latest/fernet/); [OWASP Secrets](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Secrets_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html); [qaskills](https://qaskills.sh/blog/playwright-browser-launch-args-hardening)). For Gmail, Google exempts personal-use OAuth apps from verification; a testing-mode client with the owner as sole tester avoids the CASA assessment, and an app password over IMAP remains an allowed exception with 2-Step Verification but is a long-lived full-mailbox secret that must be encrypted at rest ([Google restricted-scope verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification); [Google Admin Help](https://support.google.com/a/answer/6260879?hl=en)).

## ADHD guidance asks for one next action, dismissible reminders and no shame

The design guidance converges with unusual consistency. W3C COGA says to "provide users with five or less main choices on each screen," to give "an estimate of the amount of effort required" before multi-step tasks, to "avoid timeouts and let the user save their work as they go," to "always let the user return to a previous point," and that reminders "must be timely and dismissible" with the user deciding "how many reminders they want and of which type" ([W3C COGA](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/design_guide.html); [COGA summary](https://github.com/microsoft/hve-core/blob/main/.github/skills/accessibility/accessibility/references/frameworks/coga.md)). St Andrews' 2025 ADHD guide adds predictable layouts, small chunks, customisable notifications and animations, gentle reminders with progress indicators and auto-save, and warns against time limits "without an option to extend" ([St Andrews](https://digitalcommunications.wp.st-andrews.ac.uk/2025/02/12/designing-for-users-with-adhd/)). Shame-free design matters: streak mechanics drive ADHD users away after one missed day and an ADHD-friendly app should be "fundamentally incapable of causing a shame spiral"; "Save for Later" beats "Quit" ([Wellnest](https://wellnestapp.app/blog/adhd-medication-app-design); [Gapsy](https://gapsystudio.com/blog/mental-health-app-design/)). ADHD coaching sources recommend the "ONE Thing Rule" because a long list triggers "brain freeze," deadlines set two days early for buffer, and tracker updates paired with the submission habit ([ADHD Wisdom Tools](https://adhdwisdomtools.substack.com/p/the-one-thing-rule-the-adhd-strategy); [Coaching with Brooke](https://www.coachingwithbrooke.com/post/5-ways-to-actually-meet-deadlines-when-you-have-adhd); [Beyond BookSmart](https://www.beyondbooksmart.com/executive-functioning-strategies-blog/job-searching-with-adhd-five-executive-function-skills-to-help-make-it-easier)). The "which job was this?" problem is real: a reader called back two weeks after applying "honestly couldn't remember what the company did," and the laid-off designer who built her own sheet logged JD link, resume version sent and interview notes precisely to recover that context ([Ask a Manager](https://askamanager.org/2021/07/am-i-supposed-to-remember-the-details-of-every-job-i-apply-to.html); [Substack](https://nikitisza.substack.com/p/i-got-laid-off-and-built-a-job-tracking)). The reference point users have for notifications is LinkedIn's "incessantly sending random, irrelevant, and annoying notifications," so a nudge must be rare and tied to a real deadline or it will be lumped in ([Fibery Openion](https://fibery.io/openion/linkedin-77/persistent-nocaptcha-verification-issue-on-sign-in-and-sign-up-221292)). Nobody in the category is winning on dark mode or accessibility; no review mentions either, which makes them cheap differentiation that directly serves the "minimise distractions" guidance. The caveat is that no controlled study on countdown timers or single-focus views for ADHD job seekers exists; this is guideline and practitioner evidence, and countdowns should inform, never lock.

## The plan: a delta on top of what jobhunt already has

Jobhunt already occupies most of the empty quadrant: inbox classification including assessments, deadline countdowns with calendar alarms, legitimacy scoring, voice-learned letters, review-by-default applying with source-labelled answers and live takeover, follow-up nudges with drafts, and self-hosting. The plan therefore concentrates on three things the research says still decide trust: making the loud surfaces reach the phone, making every AI and automation action visible and provable, and making the data survive anything. Ranking is impact for an ADHD or busy user multiplied by inverse effort; S/M/L effort estimates assume the existing FastAPI + Jinja + SQLite codebase.

### Do not build

The research is equally clear about what to leave out. Do not build an unattended autopilot or a "first N without review" default; Massive's 164 and JobCopilot's 20-in-15-minutes are the refund disputes that followed ([Trustpilot Massive](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/usemassive.com)). Do not automate LinkedIn Easy Apply; LinkedIn's policy and the AIHawk restriction put the user's professional account at risk, and the channel converts worst anyway ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/90586); [Jobloo](https://jobloo.co/blog/best-day-time-to-apply-jobs-data/)). Do not chase volume; 819 applications produced five interviews, and recruiters now filter sameness with fingerprinting and Real Talent ([Job Search with AI](https://jobsearchwithai.substack.com/p/i-let-ai-apply-to-819-jobs-for-me); [Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/greenhouse-real-talent-tm-launches-to-fix-overwhelming-candidate-pipelines-while-combatting-fraud-and-spam-in-hiring)). Do not let the AI invent resume facts, "tailor" skills the user lacks, or guess visa, salary or demographic answers ([Trustpilot JobCopilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/jobcopilot.com)). Do not insert a proxy email domain between the user and employers; Massive's did and replies arrived as blank screens ([Resumly](https://www.resumly.ai/best/best-job-application-bots)). Do not add recruiter matching, resume sharing or any "present you to employers" layer ([Simplify privacy](https://simplify.jobs/privacy)). Do not build streaks, applications-vs-interviews ratios on the home screen, or any "you missed X" framing ([Wellnest](https://wellnestapp.app/blog/adhd-medication-app-design)). Do not build an always-open AI side panel or push notifications for recommendations; LinkedIn's notification spam is the reference users hate ([Fibery Openion](https://fibery.io/openion/linkedin-77/persistent-nocaptcha-verification-issue-on-sign-in-and-sign-up-221292)). Do not meter anything in credits or hide caps; every hidden limit in the research became a 1-star review ([Trustpilot LazyApply](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lazyapply.com)). Do not depend on a browser extension as the only capture path; extension permission resets and ownership transfers are the category's stability and security weak point ([LoopCV guide](https://www.loopcv.pro/guides/simplify-jobs-not-working/); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3092299/my-most-useful-chrome-extension-was-stealing-my-data-for-years.html)).

## Conclusion

The market has split along an axis that the incumbents did not choose deliberately: platforms (LinkedIn, Indeed Career Scout) are absorbing discovery, matching and coaching chat for free, while third-party tools either stay manual trackers or become distrusted bots. The durable position is the one neither side occupies, a tool that watches the inbox including spam, enforces deadlines on the phone, explains every score, shows exactly what a recruiter received, and leaves a receipt. Jobhunt is already there architecturally; the remaining work is making those guarantees visible (Phase 1) and making them survive a dead server, a stolen database or a curious platform (Phase 2). Because jobhunt has no subscription, it also sidesteps the single largest complaint class in the category outright, which means its trust budget is spent only on accuracy and transparency, and those are design choices rather than business-model constraints.

Two evidence gaps should temper confidence. Reddit was unreachable in every research pass, so community sentiment is relayed through vendor blogs, and the missed-assessment stories are from 2020-2022 Blind threads rather than in-window primary sources. Neither changes the plan's direction, but both argue for instrumenting jobhunt's own outcomes (which alerts fired, which were snoozed, which deadlines were met) so that the next revision of this plan can be driven by the user's data rather than by other products' complaints.
